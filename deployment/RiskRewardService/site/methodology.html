MONEY MARK CHARTS

Methodology

HOW THE NUMBERS WORK

Allocation follows logarithmic price position

Each chart has a lower line and an upper line. The lower line maps to a 10% allocation of invested cash and the upper line maps to 0%. Prices between them are interpolated on a logarithmic scale, matching the logarithmic price axis used by the charts.

Suggested allocationclamp(10 × ln(upper ÷ current) ÷ ln(upper ÷ lower), 0, 10)

On a logarithmic scale, halfway means the geometric midpoint—not the ordinary arithmetic midpoint. Therefore, when current = √(upper × lower), the displayed allocation is 5%.

Risk/reward position

The position shown along the colored range is the inverse view of the same calculation, reported on a 0–10 scale:

Position from lower to upperclamp(10 × ln(current ÷ lower) ÷ ln(upper ÷ lower), 0, 10)
  • 0 / 10 position: at or below the lower line; allocation is 10%.
  • 5 / 10 position: logarithmically halfway; allocation is 5%.
  • 10 / 10 position: at or above the upper line; allocation is 0%.

Personal holding targets

When a total invested amount is saved, the suggested allocation first produces an expected investment. Each stock’s multiplier then scales that result from 0.0× to 2.0×:

Target holding valuetotal invested × (suggested allocation ÷ 100) × multiplier
Target sharestarget value converted to quote currency ÷ current quote

USD/CAD conversion uses the dated Bank of Canada rate published with the latest price catalog. For a cross-listed company, the user selects the exact US or Canadian symbol held. The risk/reward allocation continues to use the chart’s primary quote, while that quote is translated into the held listing’s currency for current value, target shares, and share differences. All dollar totals are then displayed in the selected portfolio currency. Cross-currency target calculations are paused when the published rate is missing or stale.

For entered shares, current holding value equals shares held multiplied by the translated listing quote. The chart details show Total holding followed by Target holding and, when they differ, Difference. Today’s holding gain or loss equals shares held multiplied by the translated daily price change, with both amounts converted to the portfolio currency when necessary. These tiles appear only when every value required for that tile is available. The combined header total follows the same completeness rule across all entered holdings.

Share targets retain fractional precision; the display is rounded to at most four decimal places.

Target range

The global alert threshold is selected from 1 to 10 points and defaults to 3 points. A holding’s current allocation points are calculated as (current holding value ÷ total invested ÷ multiplier) × 100. The displayed point difference is suggested allocation points − current allocation points. This keeps the comparison on the same logarithmic 0–10 allocation scale instead of expressing it as a percentage of the target dollars. For a 0.0× multiplier, zero shares are on target and any nonzero holding is outside the range.

The main chart list shows how many scale points each configured holding is above or below target. It is green when the absolute point difference is within the selected threshold and red when it is outside. The selected chart repeats this value in a Points high / low tile immediately below Risk/reward position.

Shares held, multipliers, total invested cash, portfolio currency, and the tolerance are browser-local. JSON export includes all of them; importing a backup replaces the portfolio settings currently saved in that browser.

Relationship to Mark Gomes’s methodology

Mark Gomes describes using his fundamental risk/reward charts to buy nearer the green/lower line, take profits nearer the red/upper line, and size positions using his “10-point rule.” The exact equations above are this site’s implementation of that guidance; the cited article does not publish this formula.

Original source: “Get Rich On Stocks! (The Rules),” Mark Gomes Research, October 11, 2019

The calculation is descriptive, not investment advice. Market-alert rules and company-specific fundamental considerations can override mechanical position sizing in Mark’s methodology.
